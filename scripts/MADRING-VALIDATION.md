# The MADRING, as shipped — measured against its two sources

GENERATED by `scripts/madring-validate.js`. Do not edit by hand; re-run it.

Every MEASURED number below is computed at generation time from two things:
the control points inlined in `game.js` (splined exactly as the game splines
them), and the `TarmacDark` mesh of the 3D model the background is baked from.
The PUBLISHED column is the circuit's own promotional figures, reproduced only
as something to compare against.

## Where the layout comes from

Two sources, and they do not agree everywhere.

| | |
|---|---|
| Published centreline | `bacinger/f1-circuits`, `circuits/es-2026.geojson` (MIT), 116 lat/lon points, declared 5474 m |
| 3D model | "Circuito de Madring 2026 layout" by Dave Love, CC-BY-4.0 — `madring-3d/assets/madring-sketchfab/` |
| Registration | `scripts/madring-model-fit.js`: rotation 0.399°, 0.973346 m per world px, mirror none |
| Background | `images/madring-overhead.jpg`, an orthographic render of that model through that same transform |

The geodata fixes the **identity** of the lap — where the start/finish line is,
which way round it goes, what order the corners come in. The model fixes the
**path**, because the model is what the player can see: the background image is a
render of it, so anywhere the road disagrees with the model, the car is visibly
driving over painted grass.

### The 636 m disagreement

Splining the published polyline the way the game does and asking, for each of its
1280 waypoints, whether that square metre is paved in the model:

| | |
|---|---|
| Published polyline on the model's asphalt | 88.2% |
| Contiguous stretches off it (>4 waypoints) | 1 |
| — waypoints 478–628 | 37.3%–49.1% of the lap, 636 m |

That single stretch is the run into La Monumental. The polyline cuts diagonally
across the infield; the circuit carries on up the left-hand straight and loops
wider. It is not a rounding error — the two are up to
81 m apart, several road widths.

The model wins there, and — since a splice would leave two seams and two
provenances — everywhere. `scripts/madring-road-centre.js` walks the middle of
the model's asphalt along the whole lap, starting from the published polyline so
the lap keeps its identity. `madring-3d` already did the same thing for the 3D
game (`madring-3d/src/circuit/road.ts`), which is why the two now measure the
same circuit to within a metre of lap length.

## What is measured, what is inferred, and what is not here at all

### Measured, and reproducible by re-running this script

Lap length, scale, corner count and every column of the corner table, the
straights, the coverage and corridor figures, the self-clearance, the positions of
the circuit's overpasses, and the classification of every checkpoint, grid slot and
pickup against the collision mask.

### Inferred

- **Corner names and numbers.** Neither source carries them. The geodata is a bare
  polyline and the model is untagged geometry. Any name attached to a corner comes
  from matching measured geometry to the circuit's published prose, and in the
  middle of the lap that matching is weak enough not to be worth writing down.
- **The banked section is a terrain type**, not geometry. A 24% bank cannot be
  drawn from directly above, so the arc measured above is painted as `banking`.

### Not present in either source

The elevation change, the surface of the banking, the pit lane as a driveable
thing, and every speed figure. The geodata is a flat closed polyline and a length;
the model is untagged geometry with a real aerial photograph on the ground plane.

### Known limits of this check

- Coverage is measured on the **centreline**, not across the width of the band.
  The band is ~45 m wide against a real corridor that is mostly 28-30 m, so it
  necessarily overhangs; that is a deliberate playability decision, not an error.
- The corner detector is run on a curve that has already been smoothed by the
  snapping passes in `madring-road-centre.js`, so very short kinks in the real
  asphalt are not resolvable here.
- Nothing here checks that the *render* is correctly exposed or that its features
  are recognisable — only that the road and the asphalt in it coincide.
