# Circuito de Madring - geodata vs published figures

Generated by `scripts/madring-corners.js`. Everything in the MEASURED columns is
computed from `bacinger/f1-circuits` `circuits/es-2026.geojson` (MIT), 115 lat/lon
points, projected onto a local metric plane. Nothing in a MEASURED column is typed in
by hand. The PUBLISHED columns are the circuit's own promotional figures and are
reproduced here only as a target to compare against.

## Source and detection settings

| | |
|---|---|
| Source file | `circuits/es-2026.geojson`, 116 coordinates (115 distinct, spaced 8-381 m) |
| Declared length | 5474 m |
| Measured length | 5415 m (-1.1%, equirectangular projection error) |
| Net heading change | 360.0°, i.e. exactly one clockwise lap |
| Resample step | 2 m (2707 samples) |
| Curvature window | 60 m |
| Corner radius threshold | 200 m |
| Minimum corner angle | 12° |
| Merge same-direction runs closer than | 40 m |
| **Corners detected** | **22** (published 22) |

### Why these thresholds

The window is 60 m because the median spacing of the source points is 30.6 m; averaging
curvature over roughly two source segments stops a single stray vertex inventing a corner
while still resolving a 40 m corner. The threshold is a **radius**, not a raw heading
delta, so a long gentle bend is not mistaken for a corner: the sweeps along Ribera del
Sena bend through 60-70° but at 400-900 m radius, which is a straight you take flat, while
every corner in the list below drops under 160 m radius. 200 m is the gap between those
two populations.

A parameter sweep of 8,640 combinations (window 40-100 m, radius 120-300 m, hysteresis
1.0-2.5x, min angle 8-20°, merge distance 20-80 m) returns 22 corners in 18.2% of cases;
the full distribution runs 15-30 with a mode of 23 and a median of 22-23. So 22 is not a
knife-edge result produced by tuning - it sits in the middle of the plausible range. The
specific setting above was then chosen because it also reproduces the published turn
angles most closely (see the angle table).

## Where the start/finish line is

The GeoJSON's first coordinate is the natural candidate. It survives both tests:

1. **It is on the second-longest straight.** Ranking every inter-corner gap on the lap,
   the straight containing coordinate 0 comes **#2**, at 536 m
   corner-exit to corner-entry (640 m apex to apex). The published main straight is
   589 m and is explicitly described as *the second longest*, behind the 837 m Turn 3-5
   blast. The longest gap measured here is 832 m. Both the ordering and the lengths agree.
2. **The distance to the next corner matches.** From coordinate 0 to the sharpest source
   vertex of the following corner is **203 m**; published start line to Turn 1 is
   **202 m**. Coordinate 0 itself sits at a vertex whose turn angle is 0.0°, i.e. dead
   straight, as a start line should be.

Reading forward from there, the detected sequence lands the 180° banked semicircle at
position 12, ~85° corners at positions 13 and 17, a mirrored left/right pair at 18/19, a
118° corner at 20 and a 90° corner at 22 - matching La Monumental (T12), T13, T17, Curva
Norte and its mirror (T18/T19), T20 and El Parque (T22). **The file's first point is the
start/finish line, and detected corner 1 is Turn 1.** Note that this also means
`madring-centreline.js` control point `i0` is the start/finish line.

## Proportions: measured vs published

Two measurements are given for each length because "how long is a straight" depends on
where you decide the bounding corners begin and end. The **exit-to-entry** figure is the
genuinely straight part; the **apex-to-apex** figure includes both corners' transitions.
The published figure should fall between them.

## What is measured, what is inferred, and what is uncertain

### Derived from the real geodata (reproducible by re-running the script)

- Lap length 5415 m, and the 1.1% shortfall against the declared 5474 m.
- Direction of travel: net heading change 360.0° = one clockwise lap.
- Corner count (22), and every angle, direction, arc length, apex position, minimum
  radius and preceding-straight length in the corner table.
- The straight rankings, and therefore the identification of the start/finish line.
- All MEASURED columns in the proportions and angle tables.

### Inference (geometry plus the published prose, not measurement)

- **Every name in the corner table.** The GeoJSON carries no turn names or numbers, only
  a bare polyline. Names are attached by matching measured geometry to the published
  descriptions.
- The mapping is confident at both ends of the lap and weak in the middle:
  - **Confident:** corner 1 = T1 (203 m measured from the line vs 202 m published);
    corner 12 = La Monumental (the only long constant-radius semicircle on the lap,
    183° over 372 m - the banking itself is not in the data); corners 13 and 17
    (both ~85°, matching the published pair of 84° braking corners); corners 18/19 (a
    left then a right of similar size, matching "Curva Norte and a near mirror going the
    other way"); corner 20 (118° vs published 117°); corner 22 (90° vs published 90°,
    immediately before the main straight).
  - **Weak:** corners 2 through 11. Detected corner 2 is a single 131° right-hand run
    that most likely contains both T2 and T3 (Curva de Hortaleza), because the 832 m
    longest straight begins at its exit and the published 837 m blast is described as
    starting at T3. If that merge is real, one detected corner between 9 and 11 must be a
    spurious split for the numbering to still land La Monumental at 12 - the likeliest
    candidate is corner 11, a 74° left that ends only 84 m before the banked section and
    may simply be its entry. Corners labelled "(probable)" in the table carry this
    uncertainty. The alternative reading, that detected corner N is Turn N throughout,
    is also arithmetically consistent but puts the 832 m straight between T2 and T3
    rather than T3 and T5.

### Known disagreements with the published descriptions

- **Turn 20 direction.** The magnitude is an excellent match (118° measured vs 117°
  published) but the geodata makes it a **left**-hander, while the published description
  calls it a tight right. The sign convention here is verified against T22, where the
  geodata gives a 90° right and the published text also says 90° right, so the convention
  is not inverted. Either the source polyline is wrong at this point or the published
  description is.
- **Turn 8 / Turn 9 directions.** Published: T8 (El Bunker) is a sharp right and T9 is a
  fast right. In the detected sequence a sharp 96° right is followed immediately by an
  82° left and then a gentle 28° right. Whichever way the numbering is aligned, one of
  the two published "right" labels ends up on a left-hander. This is the single strongest
  reason not to trust the turn numbers in the T2-T11 stretch.
- **Turn 21.** Measured 80° against a published 71° - the worst angle error in the table.
- **Las Enlazadas (T14-T16)** are published as taken flat out, but the measured minimum
  radii there are 70-117 m, which no car takes flat. This is a resolution artefact: a
  polyline sampled every ~30 m always understates the radius of a fast sweeper. Treat
  every "min radius" figure as a lower bound, not the real corner radius.
- **La Monumental length.** Measured 372 m at the 200 m radius threshold and
  476 m at a 300 m threshold. Published sources say 550 m, or 620 m in some
  accounts. The published value sits above the measured bracket. The banking (24%) is
  invisible to this data entirely - the GeoJSON is 2D.

### Not present in the source data at all

Track width (12 m, 15 m on the main straight), the 10 m elevation change, the 5% descent
at El Bunker, the two tunnels, the banking at La Monumental, the pit lane and its entry
between T21 and T22, grandstand positions, and every speed figure. The GeoJSON is a flat
closed polyline and a length. Anything the game does with those numbers comes from the
published descriptions, not from measurement.
